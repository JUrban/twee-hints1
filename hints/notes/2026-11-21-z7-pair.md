# Z7 pair: today's late-phase default against the 20 Nov system, on all goals (21 Nov 12:48)

**Question.** On half2 from zero, P2O's late-phase options (exp44/45 and exp48) gave +241 to +800 over the 20 Nov
system in side-by-side arms. Do they help the same way on the whole benchmark, where Z6 (the 20 Nov system, 12
workers, 8 hours) reached 44,095?

## Why a pair, not one arm against Z6

Cross-time comparisons are biased by the machine's load. The abstract-work correction (exp/abscurve.py) removes only a
half to three quarters of it: P2R, a replica of P2O under a heavier load, still trailed P2O by 167-426 at equal work
(notes/2026-11-21-late-combo.md). So Z7 runs beside its own control instead of being compared with Z6 alone.

* Why: the mix floors. Each class with waiting goals keeps at least one running job (`cn_ < mix × j`, 0.6 at 6
  workers). Z7's mix line at 30 minutes, in proofs per run-hour over each class's last 300 runs: r (first runs) 6,885,
  q (retries) 77, h (head runs at 40 s) 12, d 44. Its q, h and extra d runs (59, 13 and 21 more than Z7C's d) took
  about half a worker of six for about 1% of a first run's yield, which is about the 8-9% fewer runs.
* The same happened on half2: P2O was 650 behind P2N at 30 minutes (16,453 against 17,048) and ahead from 2 hours.
  With twice the goals per worker here, the first pass lasts about twice as long as on half2, so
  the early cost lasts longer. The pair runs on to 8 hours as planned.
