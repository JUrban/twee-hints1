# The derived-rule transfer with its best runs uncapped (exp42, 20 Nov)

Written 20 Nov 2026, 11:27 UTC, at launch. Script abs/exp42.sh ([hints/exp/exp42.sh](../exp/exp42.sh)).
Follows exp41 ([notes/2026-11-20-half2-given.md](2026-11-20-half2-given.md)).

## Why

* In exp41 (half2 from zero, 2 hours), the live loop's ancestry runs proved 339 goals in 0.63 CPU h, 541 per CPU h.
  Its same-statement runs proved 143 in 1.79 CPU h, 80 per CPU h.
* Both kinds share the cap of 60 goals per cycle. 145–192 ancestry hits were still pending at the end.
* givenmatch.py `--same-cap N` (20 Nov 10:40) gives the same-statement runs their own cap. solve_new.sh's
  `GIVENARGS` passes the live loop's options.

## Set and setting

* **Set:** sets/half2 (65 originals, 25,847 goals), from zero (loops/EMPTY).
* **P2F (the arm):** abs/or_test2.sh with SET=sets/half2 and `GIVENARGS="--every 300 -j 2 --cap 200 --same-cap 20
  --no-inst"`. Otherwise solve_new.sh's defaults: 6 workers, 2 hours, the transfer every 300 s, `SCHED=`.
* **Control: exp41's P2E,** the same with the default `--cap 60` shared, 20,152 at 2 hours.
  * solve_new.sh has changed since only in comments and in GIVENARGS, whose default is the old string.
  * givenmatch.py has changed only by `--same-cap`, off by default. transfer.py only in lemma names, and in a `.ax`
    third column the live loop does not write.
  * exp40's PGE (half1) is no control here: its live loop ran without `--no-inst`.
* **CPUs:** 6 workers, the transfer's 2, and the live loop's 2. Production goes to 6 workers (ul74 J=6) and the
  re-verification to 2 for the 2 hours.

## Measures

* `abs/setcurve.py sets/half2 P2E P2F --at 10,30,60,90,120`.
* One-arm goals.
* The live loop's runs, proofs and CPU by kind (ancestry, prefix, same statement), and the pending hits per cycle.
* P2F's own extra CPU against P2E's.
* **Decision:** if P2F ≥ P2E + 185 (the noise between identical runs), the caps become solve_new.sh's default. If
  within the noise, the default stays, unless the live loop's proofs per CPU h are clearly better.
