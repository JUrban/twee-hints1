# exp51 (candidate): shorter first runs for heads at the chain level (`--head-time S`) (21 Nov 08:40)

**Question.** In exp48, P2O's lead over P2N came from its head class: 152 L1 runs (the 40 s chain level) by 3 hours,
48 proved, against P2N's 34 and 16, and a proved head's later goals followed. But those runs are expensive. Would a
20 s first run for a head prove most of the same heads at about half the cost, so twice as many heads get a run?

## Why

* P2O's L1 runs at 4 hours: 154 runs, 48 proved. The failed ones took 86.6 CPU s each (2.2 CPU s per abstract
  second: chain hints make twice the critical pairs, notes/2026-11-19-hint-cost.md) and 2.55 of class h's 2.86 CPU
  hours. The proofs came at a median of 19.8 CPU s (quartiles 8.4 and 31.7): 25 within 20 s, 36 within 30 s.
* Production's L1 runs (loops/UL/r5, 10,290 runs, 1,337 proved, failed median 152 CPU s, 3.8 CPU s per abstract
  second), replayed at a shorter abstract budget (a proof counts if its CPU time is within the budget times the
  failed runs' median rate; a failed run costs that much):

  | budget | proofs kept | CPU | proofs per CPU hour |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|
  | 10 s | 47% | 23% | 1.99x |
  | 15 s | 61% | 34% | 1.78x |
  | 20 s | 73% | 45% | 1.62x |
  | 25 s | 81% | 55% | 1.48x |
  | 30 s | 88% | 64% | 1.37x |

* A caution from 19 Nov (REPORT, "A 40 s chain level"): of ul56's 80 s chain-level proofs, 28 of the 50 found
  within 40 abstract s came within 20 s (56%). The CPU-based replay above may overstate what 20 s keeps, since a
  run's CPU per abstract second grows as it goes.
* A head whose 20 s run fails keeps its state: its next level (L2, 80 s) resumes it and runs the remaining 60 s, so
  a head that needed 20-40 s is delayed, not lost.

## Change

loop.py `--head-time S` (default off; with `--head-class`):
* A run of class h at the chain level (fresh, with chain hints) gets S abstract seconds instead of the level's
  budget, if S is smaller. Its results row gets `ht=1`, with the label L1 as before.
* loop.py now records, per goal, the budget its saved deep state has reached. With --head-time, a resume runs the
  rest of its level's budget from there (a 20 s head run, then L2: 60 s more, to 80). Without the option the
  resume budget is the increment over the previous level, as before.

## Smoke test

* loops/HTT (08:38, sets/smoke487, 2 workers, 12 minutes, `--head-class --head-time 20`): no errors, 395 solved, but
  class h never ran (with 2 workers, as in exp47's HCT).
* loops/HTT2 (08:51, the same with 6 workers, 9 minutes, against exp47's HCT2): no errors, 409 solved (HCT2 408).
  The h runs carry ht=1: of the first 30 L1 rows, 28 ht=1 (7 proved, at 1.3-19.4 CPU s; the failed ones 18-47 CPU s)
  and 2 of class d at the full 40 s (one failed at 83.9 CPU s). Mix line `h 36 32/h`. No L2 resume came within
  9 minutes, so the resume budget after a short head run is checked by code reading only (T[lev] minus the budget
  the state reached, at most T[lev] - 1).
