# exp46: a new kNN lemma counts as a new context (`--knn-ctx`) (21 Nov 00:55)

**Question.** In from-zero runs, goals whose last missing lemma is a kNN lemma (a proven goal of another original)
wait a long time for their retry (notes/2026-11-21-roots.md, latency): after Z6's first hour 56% of them were proved
over 10 minutes after the lemma, while those whose last lemma was a near sibling came at once. Does retrying them
as soon as the kNN index has the lemma make the from-zero curve rise faster? Mine.

## Why

loop.py's `--fail-order` path (solve_new.sh's, SCHED= empty) retries a failed goal fresh when `ctx(n)` (its nearest
proven siblings) changed or its hint pool grew by 1.25x. `--retry-knn` (in solve_new.sh) records each goal's kNN
lemma set at its fresh runs and rebuilds the kNN index every `--knn-refresh` seconds or 300 proofs, but the check
that the set changed (`knnch`) is used only in the learned scheduler's path. So a new kNN lemma reaches a failed goal
only when a variant or deep level happens to run it, or a sibling changes.

## Change

loop.py `--knn-ctx` (default off; lemmas.LemmaIndex.sim, the kNN score of one candidate):
* at each fresh run of a goal, its threshold: the score of the weakest of its kNN picks (0 if it got fewer than
  `--lemma-knn`);
* after each kNN rebuild, every unsolved goal with a threshold is scored against the proofs new since the previous
  rebuild; one that would score above the threshold puts the goal in `knew`;
* a goal in `knew` counts as having a new context: it joins the `--fail-order` retry list (by its failure count as
  before) and leaves the deeper levels' list until its retry; the fresh run clears it;
* DIR/knn_ctx.tsv logs each fresh run that only the kNN change caused (seconds since the start, goal); the stderr
  line after each rebuild: goals that gain a lemma, waiting, seconds.

Cost measured on Z6's final data: 1,000 unsolved goals against 300 new proofs, 0.3 s (256 of them would gain one).
